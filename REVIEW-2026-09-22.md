# StretchBreak review — September 22, 2026

> Baseline findings recorded before implementation. See [implementation status](IMPLEMENTATION-STATUS.md) for the fixes and current validation.

The app has a coherent visual style, but its holiday applicability, PTO arithmetic, and AI abuse controls need correction before it can be presented as a dependable worldwide planner.

Scope: reviewed the local source, exercised the desktop UI and a 390px mobile viewport, ran the production build, TypeScript, ESLint, and npm security audits, and reproduced selected defects against the actual functions. Retrieved the current country list and 2026 holiday feeds for the UK, Netherlands, US, Nigeria, Australia, and Canada; checked unsupported responses for India and the UAE. Compared UK regional dates with GOV.UK's JSON calendar and checked official Nigerian and Dutch guidance. This is not certification of every country/year or a penetration test of a deployed site. No paid AI requests or production mutations were performed.

## Highest-priority correctness findings

| Priority | Confirmed finding | Impact and proposed change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | Selecting October 5 and October 30, 2026 produces **2 PTO days / 30 days off** with no holidays. | `calculateEfficiency` counts every date between the first and last selections, including working days. Split selections into actual connected breaks, extend each across adjoining non-working days, and count the union. Use this same calculation for suggestions, statistics, sharing, budgets, and export. |
| High | The holiday loader removes January 1 in America/Chicago. | `new Date('2026-01-01')` is UTC midnight, which is December 31 locally. Filtering by local `getFullYear()` discards the holiday. Use validated date-only values or consistent `parseISO` handling. Reproduction reduced the UK feed from 14 to 13 entries. |
| High | “All Regions” treats every subdivision's holidays as applicable to one employee. | Selecting the UK without a region includes Scottish and Northern Irish holidays in an English plan; Australia and Canada have the same class of problem. Require a work region when needed, use readable region names, and distinguish “national only” from “browse all regions.” Reset incompatible regions when changing country. |
| High | Holiday `types` are never used to decide whether a day is off. | The Dutch 2026 feed labels Good Friday as School/Authorities/Observance and Liberation Day as School/Authorities. The US feed includes Observance, Optional, and Bank records. All currently become non-working days. Build explicit applicability rules and let users confirm their employer's holiday calendar. |
| High | Adding carryover can double-count it. | `PTOInput` sends the effective balance through `onChange`, while `PlanningConfigPanel` treats that value as annual entitlement and adds carryover again. Replaying this path gives **20 annual + 5 carryover = 30 available**, instead of 25. Separate entitlement, carryover, used, and remaining values; make carryover year-specific and allocate it against actual dates before expiry. |
| High | Optimized and AI suggestions are commonly discarded by the results view. | `HolidayPlanner` chooses basic algorithm suggestions whenever any exist. Those basic suggestions do not receive the PTO budget, company holidays, strategy, or custom timeframe. Use one validated suggestion pipeline and apply the same constraints regardless of source. |
| Medium | Suggestions and statistics disagree. | In the UI, December 21–25, 2026 in the Netherlands showed 4 PTO / 5 days off; applying it showed 4 PTO / 9 days off. The latter includes adjacent weekends, while the card does not. Define and display both full break dates and exact leave dates consistently. |
| Medium | Calendar exports have invalid all-day end dates. | ICS entries use identical DTSTART and DTEND. Google links use the last selected day as the exclusive end and collapse separate breaks into one span. Use the following date for all-day end values, split disconnected breaks, and test imports in target calendars. [RFC 5545](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc5545#section-3.6.1) defines DTEND as exclusive. |

## Holiday accuracy and international coverage

**The answer to “correct across all countries?” is no.** There are both upstream omissions and mistakes in the app's interpretation of otherwise useful source data.

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Available countries | The v3 endpoint returned 204 country/territory entries. India, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Israel were absent. India and UAE holiday requests returned HTTP 204. The app attempts JSON parsing on an empty 204 response instead of explaining unsupported coverage. |
| United Kingdom, 2026 | Raw feed dates exactly matched GOV.UK after regional filtering: England/Wales 8, Scotland 10, Northern Ireland 10. This includes Scotland's June 15 World Cup holiday and December 28 substitute Boxing Day. The app still misuses the data through default region handling and date filtering. [Official calendar](https://www.gov.uk/bank-holidays) |
| Nigeria, 2026 | The retrieved feed has no March 19 or March 20 Eid-ul-Fitr holidays, both officially declared by the Ministry of Interior. This is a confirmed upstream omission, not merely a display bug. [Official declaration](https://interior.gov.ng/federal-government-declares-thursday-19th-and-friday-20th-march-2026-as-public-holidays-to-mark-eid-ul-fitr/) |
| Netherlands, 2026 | Holiday categories distinguish dates that the app treats identically. Furthermore, official public-holiday status does not itself guarantee a day off; the employment agreement matters. [Dutch government guidance](https://www.government.nl/faq/work/public-holidays-in-the-netherlands) |
| United States, 2026 | The feed contains July 3 as the observed Independence Day, consistent with the federal calendar. It also includes regional and non-public categories. Those records cannot all be treated as universal employee days off. [OPM calendar](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/federal-holidays/) |
| Australia and Canada, 2026 | Retrieved and inspected the regional records; the default “All Regions” union is unsafe for personal leave planning. Full jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction date and employment-policy validation was not performed. |

Additional international limitations:

- Saturday/Sunday weekends and a Monday–Friday work pattern are hard-coded. Support configurable working weekdays, with country/region defaults that users can override.
- Calendar week starts, month formatting, and language are fixed to English/Sunday-first. Season filters should reflect hemisphere or an explicit user preference.
- Past holidays are removed before rendering calendars, including saved-plan calendars. Historical plans therefore lose holiday context. Filter recommendation candidates by today without deleting facts from the calendar.
- Saved plans lack a region field, and reopening them uses current holiday/configuration state in several places. Persist region, workweek, company holidays, and holiday-source version with each plan, then deliberately refresh/reconcile it.
- Offline holiday cache has no visible age or expiry. Display source, retrieval date, stale status, and whether a holiday is confirmed, estimated, or employer-dependent.

Recommended holiday architecture: normalize provider data into date-only records with actual/observed date, subdivision, category, official source, verification date, and confidence. Add government-backed overrides for announced and movable holidays. Keep the provider behind an adapter; its current documentation describes a v4 schema with changed field names, so any migration needs contract tests. Maintain reference fixtures for every advertised country/region/year, covering substitute days, year boundaries, time zones, and differing workweeks. A successful API response alone does not establish accuracy. [Provider documentation](https://nagerholidays.com/api)

## UI/UX changes

The teal palette and card styling are consistent. I would retain that visual identity and reorganize the task flow.

1. **Get users to useful results sooner.** The desktop header, toolbar, navigation, and setup introduction consume most of the first screen. Reduce the header and place country, named region, year, and annual PTO together. Collapse carryover, employer holidays, templates, and wishlist under optional settings. Put “Find breaks” next to the essentials.
2. **Keep one primary workflow:** configure → preview three useful breaks → select/edit dates → save → export. “Apply Strategy” currently saves plans and consumes their PTO balance, which is surprising when someone is exploring. Keep exploration in a draft; make saving explicit and reversible.
3. **Fix calendar focus.** After applying a December break the calendar remained on January, where selected dates were invisible. Open at the current month for this year, and move to the applied selection. Add a short list of selected leave dates alongside the calendar.
4. **Make dates operable by keyboard and assistive technology.** `CalendarDay` renders a clickable div without keyboard handlers, tab stops, or selected-state semantics. Use a calendar grid with buttons, arrow-key navigation, full date/status labels, and clear focus. Do not rely solely on color or hover titles for holiday details. [CalendarDay](/Users/potus-mac/Documents/personal-work/planner/src/components/Calendar/CalendarDay.tsx:53)
5. **Repair mobile overflow.** At a 390px viewport the page was 411px wide, driven by the country controls. Constrain native selects and flex children, stack labels/controls, and simplify navigation. Calendar cells were cramped; provide a readable agenda alternative and adequately sized touch targets.
6. **Keep filters visible on zero results.** Selecting January on the remaining-2026 Bridge Board removed the entire board, including “Clear all.” Render an empty message with reset controls instead of returning null. [Bridge Board](/Users/potus-mac/Documents/personal-work/planner/src/components/BridgeBoard/BridgeBoard.tsx)
7. **Validate PTO while typing.** Entering 20 left the primary button disabled until the input blurred. Also rename “Available PTO” to “Annual allowance” if that is what the field saves, and show used/remaining separately.
8. **Replace developer copy with user-facing states.** The production UI can tell visitors to run `npm run dev:full` and configure an API key. Show “AI is temporarily unavailable” with retry instead. The AI readiness probe currently reports availability before checking key configuration and treats many JSON errors as available.
9. **Make sharing language accurate.** “Public vacation calendar” is an encoded snapshot link, and turning its switch off cannot revoke existing links. Rename it “Share a plan snapshot,” explain who can read it, and show a share preview. Replace jargon such as “not a real ACL.”
10. **Make restore transactional.** “Merge” overwrites entire stored collections rather than merging plans by ID; “Replace” clears storage before fully validating/writing the replacement. Validate everything first, preserve a rollback copy, merge by record, and report success only after all writes succeed. [Backup service](/Users/potus-mac/Documents/personal-work/planner/src/services/backupService.ts:116)

## Security and the AI feature

Existing protections worth retaining: the OpenAI key is accessed only by server functions; `.env` is ignored and is not tracked in the current checkout; React renders chat text as text; security headers are configured; request/history/output lengths are bounded; import validation and ICS escaping exist; webhook destinations are allowlisted and webhook credentials are excluded from backups. Deployed headers, historical secret exposure, and account settings were not verified.

**High priority — protect paid AI endpoints from abuse.** Origin validation is not authentication: a direct HTTP client can supply an allowed Origin, and localhost origins are allowed in production code. All three paid endpoints are anonymous. Add verified user sessions for paid usage, or a deliberate anonymous tier with server-verified bot challenges, short-lived sessions, and strict quotas. Keep CORS as an additional browser boundary.

**High priority — enforce shared limits.** Rate counters are module-level Maps, so they do not coordinate across instances/functions and reset on cold starts. The code prefers `x-forwarded-for` over Netlify's connection-IP header. A local guard simulation allowed 12 requests from the same trusted connection IP when the forwarded header varied; whether that header can be controlled in deployment still needs an edge-level check. Use platform rate limiting or an atomic shared counter with trusted client identity, per-user and per-IP limits, concurrency limits, and `Retry-After` responses. [AI guard](/Users/potus-mac/Documents/personal-work/planner/netlify/functions/_shared/aiGuard.js:121)

**High priority — make the budget cutoff fail closed.** The existing conditional-write reservation design is useful, but any Blobs error falls back to per-instance memory. A storage outage or configuration failure can therefore allow spending beyond the intended shared $10 cap. Deny new paid calls if usage cannot be durably reserved; alert on storage/accounting errors; reconcile successful usage; expire stranded reservations safely; and verify the cutoff under concurrency in staging. Handlers also ignore unsuccessful commit results. [Budget accounting](/Users/potus-mac/Documents/personal-work/planner/netlify/functions/_shared/aiGuard.js:158)

**High priority — validate AI inputs and results as untrusted data.** Suggestion responses are extracted with a regex, parsed as an array, and checked only for a plausible future start-date prefix. There is no complete validation of end dates, range length, PTO counts, holiday membership, year, or efficiency. Use strict schemas with a compatible Structured Outputs model, then independently verify dates and recompute all numeric claims. Keep planning arithmetic deterministic; have the model explain or rank valid candidates. Structured Outputs still require semantic checks. [Handler](/Users/potus-mac/Documents/personal-work/planner/netlify/functions/generate-ai-suggestions.js:157), [OpenAI documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs)

**Reduce prompt-injection exposure.** The chat handler embeds client-supplied holiday names directly into the system prompt. Removing control characters and truncating strings does not make their instructions trustworthy. Fetch authoritative holiday context server-side, separate user data from instructions, and avoid giving this feature action-taking tools. The current handlers have no shell, email, or arbitrary tool execution, so the demonstrated concern is unreliable output and paid misuse, not proven server takeover. Add adversarial-input evaluation and bounded input/output handling. [OpenAI safety guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/safety-best-practices)

**Improve privacy and credential handling.** Plan descriptions/dates are base64-encoded into query strings, which can reach hosting logs and browser history and remain readable by anyone with the URL. Prefer a minimal URL-fragment snapshot for local sharing, or authenticated expiring/revocable server links for controlled sharing; a fragment is not encryption. Webhook URLs are bearer secrets stored in localStorage: stop persisting them by default or keep them in a protected backend tied to an authenticated user. “Reset All” only clears plans/PTO, leaving team entries, versions, preferences, and webhook settings; implement a true app-data reset with explicit scope.

**Control AI costs and disclosure deliberately.** Suggestions are automatically requested after holidays load. Make AI optional and user-initiated; say what will be sent; send only required fields; cache equivalent requests; cancel or ignore stale country/year/region responses. Set explicit server timeouts and bounded retries. Add operational metrics for cost, latency, quota blocks, and storage failures without logging sensitive prompts. Use a dedicated production OpenAI project/key, separate development credentials, least privilege, MFA for hosting/source/API accounts, billing alerts, and an application-level kill switch. Rotate credentials if exposure is suspected; this review did not establish a leak.

**Patch dependencies and enforce release checks.** npm audit reported 24 affected packages: 20 high, 3 moderate, 1 low. Most are in the development/build tree. The production-only audit still reported `image-size@2.0.2`, reached through `@netlify/blobs → @netlify/dev-utils`; its advisories concern malicious image parsing denial of service. No reachable image-upload exploitation path was demonstrated in this app. Update the affected dependency chain and review runtime reachability rather than treating every advisory as an exposed production vulnerability. [ICNS advisory](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-w3rx-r6r6-pgpr), [JXL/HEIF advisory](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-5p2g-fcmc-qvqq)

The service worker also caches the HTML shell first with a fixed cache version. New releases can remain hidden behind old cached HTML unless the cache version changes. Use a deliberate release/update strategy so security fixes reach installed clients. [Service worker](/Users/potus-mac/Documents/personal-work/planner/public/sw.js)

## Validation and recommended order

- Production Vite build: passed using Node 22; output was written outside the repository.
- TypeScript: failed with 19 diagnostics, including conflicting `SuggestionFilters` declarations and unused symbols.
- ESLint: 83 errors and 7 warnings, including hook/state/immutability findings.
- No automated test script is configured. The production build runs Vite without TypeScript checking.
- Targeted reproductions confirmed date filtering, disconnected-break arithmetic, carryover double-counting, UI focus, empty-filter disappearance, and mobile overflow.

First fix holiday applicability and date-only handling, shared PTO/break arithmetic, and paid-endpoint protections. Next fix export, saved-plan context, AI schema validation, and dependency findings. Then simplify onboarding, make the calendar accessible/responsive, and add CI regression tests around these failures. Only advertise verified country/region coverage; display limitations for the rest.
